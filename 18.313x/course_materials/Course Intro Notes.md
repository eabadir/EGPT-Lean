- LET'S GET INTO THE MATERIAL A NEW EGPT STORY --
You've looked briefly at EGPT's definitions of Numbers and they look bizarre? How can a Natural number be a so called ParticlePath. Why is a ParticlePath a vector and not a scalar? 

It's time for you to become Stan. Project yourself back to 1943 and your best friend Johnny desparately wants you meet with him to recruit you to some secret project he's working on. He won't tell you much about it but he says they're going to build the first electronic computer and solve quantum mechanics problems on it - sounds interesting you do love the mysteries of waves but You know almost nothing about computers except that they seem to be glorified punch card machines and good god are they slow ... 
Johnny wants to meet at the world famous Cafe 49 in Manhattan, he does love his food! His belly is almost as big as his brain! You suspect that Johnny is going out on a limb for you on this one you're sure everyone from Fermi to Bohr are already on board and you need to give Johnny ammunition for why you belong in the room. Why do they even need an electronic computer if they have Johnny, he is a walking calculus machine! Oh, you realize, they need it for everyone else.

But first things first, where the heck is Cafe 49 was not so famous in Lwow and definitely didn't compare to the Scottish Cafe so you look it up and find out it is on 7th St and Avenue of the America ... you wonder why they called it Cafe 49 but you immediately see that on the grid of Manhattan, Avenue of the America's is also the 7th Avenue ... ha ha, very clever you think. 

Before you write down the address, you decide that if you're going to be talking about computers you better start thinking like one. No way are you going to waste your time playing with punch cards machines - you live the life of the mind. You do what you always do, you go to the primary sources and you read them. You've heard that JvM's friend Claude Shannon wrote a brilliant master's thesis about relay switching and boolean circuits (AND, OR, or NOT) and that's where you are going to start.  You're new self appointed job is to take everything you see and figure out how to make holes in cards. But what is a hole or the absense of one? What is an electronic hole in a virtual punch card. JvM's friend Shannon's more recent writing says it's basically Morse code. It is a string of 1's and 0's. 

It's going to be a few days till meet Johnny at the Scottish Cafe and you know you'll probably forget the address so you need to write it down where you're going to meet Johnny. You don't want to forget and miss your big chance!

Dedicated as you are to thinking like a computer, and in your never ending laziness, you will not write down 7th ave and 7th street. But how are you going to write it in ones and zeros and still remember what it means? It is a unique address - you can start at 0,0 and walk to 7th ave turn left and walk up to 7th St.. Do you write that as 49 1's? That's obviously inefficient. You are well aware of Leibnitz's binary system and you could write it as 110001. Even if you were willing to do the mental work of decoding that, will you remember that this some how represents 7th Ave and 7th St? What if JvM changes the address on you? You are so lazy that it is a cardinal sin to throw away information once you have done the work to gain it - it was a pain finding that map! No, this won't do.

You need a system that preserves the information of where you are going and that always works. You think back to how you looked at the map to find 7th Ave and 7th St in the first place. You thought about how you would walk there ... would you go to 1st Ave turn left, walk up one to 1st St and turn right to 2nd Ave, etc.. That would be 14 decisions (2 for each diagnoal). Or you could walk to 7th Ave, turn once and walk to 7th st. That seems better but - you are Stan, you are lazy! You think about taking a taxi and letting the taxi choose at random - for all you care the taxi can take any one of the 128 different paths to get there.  

You realize a big problem now. The way you write down the address will play a big part in how many AND, OR, NOT decisions this hypothetical computer thing is going to make. You sure wish you could talk to that Shannon kid. And then it hits you - you aren't writing down two numbers. You are writing a code that to send yourself a message in the future. What is the shortest code that preserves your message? What if you encoded a 1 for every avenue and a 0 for every street?
